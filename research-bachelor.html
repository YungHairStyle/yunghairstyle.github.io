Honours research · Unpublished manuscripts

Statistical mechanics through computational experiments

Explore Boltzmann distributions, equilibrium, and entropy through controlled simulations of dice systems.

Concordia University · With Saurabh Maiti · Honours thesis 2025; manuscript work 2026

The idea

A system of dice offers a concrete way to explore the statistical structure behind thermodynamics. Constrain a total value, distinguish configurations from measured distributions, and ask which distributions are favored and how two subsystems approach equilibrium.

My honours research used numerical demonstrations to connect these questions with entropy and statistical mechanics. The work led to two manuscripts with Saurabh Maiti, organized around Boltzmann distributions and equilibrium, followed by exchange processes and the second law.

My contribution

  • Developed simulations and numerical visualizations of constrained dice ensembles and interacting subsystems.
  • Produced and refined the figures supporting the manuscripts, making the numerical comparisons and equilibrium behavior visible.
  • Connected the statistical-mechanics investigation with separate exploratory work on restricted Boltzmann machines.

What this work demonstrates

This is a computational and pedagogical investigation of established statistical principles. It demonstrates simulation, numerical reasoning, and scientific visualization; it does not claim a new law of thermodynamics. The manuscripts are unpublished and are presented as research examples, separately from my published work.

The restricted-Boltzmann-machine demonstrations are a related exploratory project and are not presented as results of these two manuscripts.
