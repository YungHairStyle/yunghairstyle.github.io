Accuracy and resources in quantum algorithms

How do approximation choices translate into the accuracy and resource costs of a quantum circuit?

Concordia University · Supervisor: Abhijeet Alase · 2025–present

The computational question

Many quantum algorithms need a circuit that applies a function of an operator: time evolution, for example, or an inverse used in a linear-system solver. Different constructions can implement similar transformations while making different demands on oracle calls, auxiliary qubits, normalization, and classical preprocessing.

My MSc work studies these trade-offs for functions of unitary operators. I develop numerical comparisons alongside mathematical analysis, with the goal of understanding both achievable accuracy and the costs of obtaining it.

A concrete benchmark

One benchmark starts from a small unitary U and defines the Hermitian operator H = (U + U†)/2. The associated function on the unit circle is:

f(eiθ) = e−it cos θ
so that f(U) = e−itH.

This gives a common target for phase-estimation-based interpolation, linear combinations of unitaries, and generalized quantum signal processing. At the scalar level, I also compare ordinary and averaged interpolation with Jacobi–Anger truncation and numerical minimax approximations.

My contribution

  • Computational framework: implement circuit constructions and classical approximation routines in Python, NumPy, SciPy, and Qiskit.
  • Numerical validation: extract the block obtained with auxiliary registers initialized and projected onto zero, then compare it with a directly computed matrix target.
  • Resource accounting: count calls to U and U†, including controlled calls, while tracking block normalization separately.
  • Research formulation: identify where approximation degree, circuit error, and implementation overhead enter a fair comparison.

What the comparison measures

For a circuit encoding B ≈ f(U)/α, one diagnostic is the rescaled operator-norm error ‖αB − f(U)‖. I retain raw query counts and α separately; α times the query count is a comparison diagnostic, rather than a complete runtime estimate. The actual Laurent degree is also distinguished from each construction’s internal parameter.

Current status

The research direction and computational tools are established; bounds, optimality questions, and comparisons across function classes remain ongoing. Small-matrix simulations test particular implementations. Dense-grid approximation errors and numerical minimax solves are estimates, not certified uniform bounds or proofs of asymptotic optimality.
