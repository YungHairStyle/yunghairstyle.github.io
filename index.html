Accuracy and resources in quantum algorithms

Operator functions · Laurent approximation · Hamiltonian simulation

Many quantum algorithms use the implementation of a function of a unitary operator as a building block. I investigate how approximation choices and circuit constructions affect the accuracy and computational resources of these primitives.

My current work combines mathematical analysis and computational comparisons, keeping approximation error, query complexity, and implementation overhead distinct. Complexity bounds and optimality questions are ongoing research directions.

Green’s functions for degenerate Hubbard ground states

Many-body physics · Qiskit · Classical benchmarks

I extended a Liouvillian-recursion workflow to small odd-site Hubbard models. Working with the team, I implemented fermionic-swap circuits connecting spin-related ground states and combined pure-state contributions to evaluate a ground-state ensemble without directly preparing a mixed state.

The work included exact-diagonalization benchmarks, simulator calculations, IBM quantum-hardware runs, and exploration of symmetry-based qubit tapering.

A closer look at the project

The computational question

The target ensemble was an equal mixture of two degenerate ground states. By linearity, its expectation values can be obtained by averaging separate pure-state expectation values. Spin-exchange symmetry provided a way to generate the second state from a circuit preparing the first.

Implementation and checks

I implemented the required fermionic-mode permutation, integrated the circuits into three- and five-site calculations, and analyzed Green’s functions and Galitskii–Migdal energy estimates against classical benchmarks.

Scope and limitations

This was an extension of an existing workflow for specific small systems. The internship report records three-site Green’s-function convergence at roughly ten recursion steps; computational cost limited five-site calculations before full convergence. The project did not establish quantum advantage or a general solution to ground-state preparation.
