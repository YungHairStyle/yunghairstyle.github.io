Research internship · Completed 2026

Green’s functions with degenerate ground states

Use symmetry and separate pure-state measurements to evaluate a degenerate ground-state ensemble.

AlgoLabs, Université de Sherbrooke · Supervisor: Simon Verret · Summer 2026

The problem

I extended an existing Liouvillian-recursion workflow for many-body Green’s functions to three- and five-site Hubbard models. In the systems studied, the target was an equal mixture of two degenerate ground states, while the available variational preparation produced a pure state.

Preparing a mixed state was not necessary for the required observables. Linearity gives:

ρ = ½(|g₁⟩⟨g₁| + |g₂⟩⟨g₂|)
Tr(ρO) = ½(⟨g₁|O|g₁⟩ + ⟨g₂|O|g₂⟩)

The practical question was how to access the second ground state and integrate both contributions into the existing calculation.

The implementation

The two states are related by exchanging the spin-up and spin-down fermionic modes. I implemented the required mode permutation using adjacent fermionic-swap gates, retaining the fermionic signs that ordinary qubit swaps can miss.

01 / PREPAREOne ground stateUse a variational circuit for a pure-state representative.
02 / TRANSFORMIts spin partnerExchange fermionic mode blocks with adjacent FSWAPs.
03 / COMBINEEnsemble observablesAverage the separately evaluated pure-state contributions.

My contribution

  • Integrated the two ground-state circuits into three- and five-site calculations, using Jordan–Wigner mappings with six and ten qubits respectively.
  • Checked the ground-state energies, overlap, and symmetry transformation in simulation.
  • Computed Green’s functions and Galitskii–Migdal energy estimates, comparing with exact-diagonalization benchmarks.
  • Worked with noiseless simulations and IBM Quebec quantum-hardware runs, and investigated symmetry-based qubit tapering as an alternative reduction strategy.

Results and scope

The internship report records three-site Green’s-function convergence at roughly ten recursion steps. Resource demands limited the five-site calculations before full Green’s-function convergence. The work supplied a usable extension for these systems, rather than a general ground-state preparation method or a demonstration of quantum advantage.

Qubit tapering was a useful investigation, but the final ensemble treatment relied on the spin-related states and their separate contributions.
